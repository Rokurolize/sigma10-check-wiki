I believe, like many things, it starts with a story.

A story of two brothers, sitting in a lonely field, staring off into the sunset. Along the horizon dotted countless windmills, rotating against the push and pull of the Midwestern wind. I can remember us counting all of the windmills that never moved; us theorizing why they weren't active. Did they not have enough wind?

But that was long ago, and things are different now. Much more different.

My brother lost his sight 3 years ago, in a chemical accident. The changes to our lives were extreme. He always prided himself on his independence — a topic we often try our best to joke about today. He used to talk about wanting to climb up one of those windmills someday, maybe to appreciate the view or even just understand what it feels like. To be so high above the ground.

[[include component:image-block name=https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/local--files/theme:enlightened-eyes/turbines.jpg|caption=Look at the turbines.|width=400px]]

One time we stopped by our spot in that field, and he looked at me, as if something became so clear to him.

"Have you seen them?" He asked with a smile.

"The 'mills?" I replied. Was this some kind of joke?

"You're not paying attention!" He grabbed my head and forced my eyes closed. I tried to resist, tell him off, but he held his finger to my mouth and whispered his question again.

"Have you seen them?"

I'm not really sure what happened next. It's like my mind forgot where my eyes were. All I could see were the countless colors and lines dancing inside my eyelids. Again and again I witnessed pulsating shapes, exotic hues, and alien creatures that I could never hope to describe to anyone else.

And in the midst of all that was when I noticed it. A group of flocking bird-like creatures, high in the sky. Their wings, sharper than razorblades, rotating eccentrically around their torso. I could feel their eyes on me--each one a fixed, unblinking point of attention, waiting.

I was absolutely horrified.

I turned to my brother again, who only smiled and held his finger to his own lips. "Be quiet," he whispered. "Those who see will be safe."

I opened my eyes and the windmills appeared again. This time, however, I noticed something odd. The turbines had a new shape that I had previously never noticed. Between their spinning blades, I could make out an outline of a large pupil, staring off into the distance.

And that's when it started to click, I think. When I realized that the world around me was nothing like the world that hides from me.

How I had to blind myself to witness what really exists beyond my sights.

[[div class="blockquote"]]

Now that you've seen the above, let's break these down into more digestible concepts. To begin, it's important to note that there are actually two systems at play here, the **Parawatch Header** and the **Parawatch Footer**. If you want your post to work, both of these need to be included first:

[[div class="blockquote"]]
@@[[include :scp-wiki:component:parawatch-post start=--@@
[...]
]]

@@--@@> Here's where your cool text should go! <@@--@@

@@[[include :scp-wiki:component:parawatch-post end=--@@
[...]
]]
[[/div]]

Between those components is where your post text should go. The {{[...]}} are shorthand for the various configurations that both the header and footer are capable of. Let's get into what those look like now.

[[/div]]

[[div class="blockquote"]]
+++ Parawatch Header

[[collapsible show="Show Special Flags" hide="Hide Flags"]]

[[=]]
[[<]]
[[code]]

| _conclude=
| _log=
| _censor=
[[/code]]
[[/<]]
[[/=]]

These are formatting flags that can change the appearance of your post. These are turned off by default. If you wish to have a specific format, repeat the flag on the opposite side of the equals sign. For example: {{_conclude=conclude}}, or {{_log=log}}.

{{_conclude}}: This formats the Parawatch post to include a highlighted accent line underneath it, making it appear to “end” a thread or post. Useful for multi-thread conversations or when you need to signify the end of a post.
{{_log}}: This formats the Parawatch post body, as seen in the above example by the bot **FLOM.**
{{_censor}}: This adds black horizontal bars across the body of a Parawatch post, as seen in the example. These are useful for spoilers or other hidden details.

[[/collapsible]]

[[collapsible show="Show Settings" hide="Hide Settings"]]

[[=]]
[[<]]
[[code]]

| depth=
| color=
| emoji=
| username=
| date=
| postno=
| badge=
[[/code]]
[[/<]]
[[/=]]

These are the various settings you'll have to specify. Some of these can be left blank depending on which toggles you enabled/disabled. Let's go through each of these:
* {{depth=}} Specifies the depth of the post. Can range from {{one}} to {{five}}. Leave this field empty if no depth is needed.
* {{color=}} Changes the primary accent color of the post. Accepts base colors, RGB, and HEX values.
* {{emoji=}} If you enabled this setting, you can add your emoji here. Use the option "none" if you don't want an emoji.
* {{username=}} The username of the Parawatch user who made the post.
* {{date=}} The date of the post made.
* {{postno=}} The post number, which appears nearby the badge.
* {{badge=}} The badge value, which could range from things like {{OP}} or {{ADMIN}}. I'd recommend keeping these as short as possible. If you don't want a badge value, simply use "none" as the option (e.g., {{badge=none}}).

These control the contents of the footer. All of these support "none" as an option. If you use "none" for all of them, the footer will not display.
* {{tags=}} Specifies the tags underneath the post. Any space-separated values can be used here, like {{#tag1}} {{#tag2}} etc.
* {{upvotes=}} You can add a number here which will show in the upvotes section of the footer.
* {{downvotes=}} Same thing as the upvotes, but less happy.
